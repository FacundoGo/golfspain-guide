How to Get a Golf Handicap in Spain (2026)

From your first lesson to an official Handicap Index — the steps, what it costs, and how to bring your number down.

📅 Updated September 2026 ✅ Checked against RFEG sources ⏱ 6 min read
Quick Answer

Take a few lessons with a golf professional at a club. When the pro sees you can play safely and know the basic rules and etiquette, they certify you. You then take out a federation licence (licencia federativa) through the club — €90.30 for adults in 2026, plus a small regional fee — and you receive your official Handicap Index. From there you lower it by handing in scores.

In Spain, a handicap and a federation licence go together. The Royal Spanish Golf Federation (RFEG) runs the World Handicap System here, and your Handicap Index lives on your licence. You don't apply to the federation directly: a federated club — and usually one of its golf professionals — is your way in.

Step by step

  1. Take lessons with a golf professional. Most clubs run beginner courses. Besides the swing, you learn the basic rules and etiquette: pace of play, safety, repairing pitch marks, raking bunkers.
  2. Get certified by the pro. When the professional is happy that you can get round a course safely and know the rules, they certify you. Some regional federations run an exam day instead.
  3. Take out your federation licence. The club processes it for you with the regional federation and the RFEG. If you don't belong to a club, you can apply as an independent player through your regional federation.
  4. Receive your Handicap Index. It appears in the free RFEGolf app, which is also your digital licence card.
  5. Start handing in scores to bring it down (see below).
From my experience: I went to a class, the instructor saw I could play decently and knew the basic rules and etiquette, and he approved me for a handicap of 36. The licence cost me somewhere between €80 and €120 for the year.

What handicap will I start with?

The World Handicap System goes up to 54.0, and new players with no scores often start there. In practice many clubs and professionals start beginners at 36, as happened to me. Either way, your number starts moving as soon as you hand in scores.

How to lower your handicap

Club competitions are the classic route: every competition you play in is registered and your index updates automatically.

Since the World Handicap System arrived, some clubs also allow general-play rounds (vueltas valederas) to count. You declare the round before teeing off, play with a marker, and submit the card in the RFEGolf app the same day — late cards can't be added. Whether this is allowed, and under what conditions, is up to your club, so ask them.

Want a rough idea first? Put your recent scores — 9 or 18 holes — into our handicap calculator. It shows every step of the WHS formula, so you can see where your number would land before you federate.

Visiting or moving to Spain

Visiting: you don't need a Spanish licence. Bring proof of your handicap from your home club or federation — many Spanish courses ask for it. If you play occasionally without an annual licence, some clubs add a small day licence (it mainly covers insurance) to the green fee.

Moving here: ask your new Spanish club to take out your licence and carry over your existing index, rather than starting again.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a golf licence cost in Spain?

The RFEG adult licence with handicap costs €90.30 for 2026, plus a regional federation fee of roughly €10–18. Expect about €100–110 a year in total.

Can a golf professional give me a handicap?

A professional at a federated club can certify that you are ready to play. With that certificate the club processes your federation licence, and you receive your official Handicap Index through the RFEG.

What handicap do beginners start with in Spain?

The World Handicap System goes up to 54.0 and new players often start there, but many clubs and professionals start beginners at 36.

How can I lower my handicap in Spain?

By handing in scores: club competitions count, and some clubs also let general-play rounds count if you declare them beforehand and submit the card in the RFEGolf app the same day.

Do I need a Spanish licence to play golf in Spain as a tourist?

No. Visitors can play with a handicap certificate from their home club or federation. Some clubs add a small day licence to the green fee for players without an annual licence.
