Best Golf Trip Destinations in Spain (2026)

Costa del Sol, Valencia & Costa Blanca, Catalonia or the Canaries? How the four main regions compare on season, green fees and what kind of trip they suit.

📅 Updated October 2026 ✅ Green fees checked against club sources ⏱ 7 min read
Quick Answer

First golf trip to Spain: the Costa del Sol: more than 60 courses within a short drive of Málaga airport.

Best value / city break: Valencia, Alicante & Castellón: good courses from about €40–€160 for 18 holes.

Scenery + Barcelona: Catalonia. Guaranteed winter sun: the Canary Islands.

Spain has over 400 golf courses and four distinct regions that cover almost any kind of trip: a long weekend with mates, a week with the family, or a winter escape from colder fairways back home. Here's how the main options actually compare, so you can pick a region instead of just a course.

Costa del Sol (Málaga): the "Costa del Golf"

Spain's original golf tourism hub earned its nickname for a reason: more than 60 courses within a short drive of Málaga airport, many designed by names like Seve Ballesteros, Robert Trent Jones and Cabell Robinson.

  • Best for: first-timers to golf in Spain, and groups who want courses within 20–30 minutes of each other
  • Season: playable nearly year-round; peak is October–May, when the heat elsewhere in Spain has dropped
  • Standouts: Valderrama (host of the 1997 Ryder Cup), Finca Cortesín, La Reserva de Sotogrande
  • Green fees: Valderrama €600 (Monday–Thursday only, with a mandatory forecaddie at about €70 on top, so budget €700+ all-in)1; Finca Cortesín from €350 (dynamic pricing)2; La Reserva de Sotogrande quotes on request, with packages bundling it with other Sotogrande courses from about €730 per person for a multi-night stay3
Watch for: the best-known clubs book up fast and charge premium green fees in winter. Reserve tee times 4–6 weeks out for December–February.

Valencia, Alicante & Castellón

Less famous than the Costa del Sol but better value, with well-kept clubs across all three provinces: Valencia city and its inland foothills, the Costa Blanca around Alicante, and a quieter cluster up in Castellón. This is the region our course directory covers in detail, with verified green fees and booking links.

  • Best for: good golf without Costa del Sol pricing, or combining golf with a city trip to Valencia or Alicante
  • Season: similar year-round playability to the Costa del Sol, slightly hotter in high summer
Watch for: courses are more spread out than on the Costa del Sol, so plan extra driving time if you're hopping between provinces on a multi-course week.

Valencia province

  • El Saler: Javier Arana's 1968 links between the Mediterranean and the Albufera, regularly ranked among Europe's best. €115 booking ahead online, €150 rack rate4
  • Escorpión: private club, invited guests only, around €160 (confirm with the club before visiting)5
  • Foressos: €70 weekday / €80 weekend for 18 holes5, one of the best-value 18-hole courses near Valencia city
  • Oliva Nova (Seve Ballesteros design, on the Valencia/Alicante border): €120 for 18 holes, or €65 a round with the 10-round pack (€650), good news for groups6

More options, including 9-hole courses from €10, in our cheapest golf in Valencia guide.

Alicante province (Costa Blanca)

  • Alicante Golf (Playa de San Juan): €50 low/mid season, €75 high season, €32 twilight7
  • Club de Golf Ifach (Benissa, 9 holes): €49.50 for 18 holes, with a €39 July–August afternoon promo8, one of the cheaper reliable options on the Costa Blanca
  • La Marquesa (Rojales): €71–€87 depending on season9
  • Las Colinas and La Sella: the region's premium resort courses use dynamic online pricing, from about €68 at Las Colinas and around €100 at La Sella5

Castellón province

  • Mediterráneo Golf (Borriol): €80 weekday / €95 weekend for 18 holes10, the most established club in the province
  • Costa de Azahar (Grao de Castellón, 9 holes, next to the beach): €24–€30 for 9 holes, €40–€45 for 1811

Catalonia (Girona & the Costa Brava)

A quieter golf scene with a different character: rolling, tree-lined courses inland, with coastal towns nearby if you want non-golf days built into the trip.

  • Best for: combining golf with a Barcelona trip, or groups who want scenery over course density
  • Season: best April–June and September–October; summer can be hot inland, winter cooler than the south
  • Standouts: Camiral Golf (formerly PGA Catalunya, 2031 Ryder Cup venue), Golf Costa Brava, and Real Club de Golf El Prat near Barcelona
  • Green fees: Camiral's Stadium Course €182–€249, its Tour Course from €9212; Club de Golf Costa Brava €114 for 18 holes in high season (March–October), €87 in low season (November–December)13

Canary Islands

The honest answer for anyone chasing guaranteed winter sun. Year-round spring temperatures make the islands the base for a lot of Northern European golfers' winter trips.

  • Best for: winter trips (November–March), when mainland Spain can still get cold spells
  • Season: genuinely playable 12 months a year
  • Standouts: Real Club de Golf de Las Palmas (Gran Canaria), Golf Costa Adeje (Tenerife)
  • Green fees: Las Palmas from €78 for 9 holes14; Golf Costa Adeje's Championship Course €110 for 18 holes in peak season, €73 in summer (June–August), plus the 9-hole Los Lagos course from €2115
Watch for: flights and hotels cost more in peak winter weeks (Christmas, February half-term) than on the mainland.

Building the trip

  1. Group size changes the math. A group of 4+ can usually negotiate package rates directly with a club or a local golf-travel agent, and some clubs sell multi-round packs (Oliva Nova's 10-round pack drops the price from €120 to €65 a round). Solo players and pairs are often better off booking green fees online.
  2. Mix a "name" course with one or two lesser-known ones. Most trip budgets get eaten by green fees at the famous clubs. Balance one or two bucket-list rounds with cheaper local courses to keep the week's average down.
  3. Bring your handicap certificate. Many Spanish clubs ask for a valid handicap card at check-in, especially the more exclusive courses. Not sure where you stand? Our handicap calculator shows the strokes you'd get at each course.

Frequently asked questions

Where is the best place in Spain for a golf trip?

For a first trip with lots of courses close together, the Costa del Sol. For better value and a city break, Valencia, Alicante and Castellón. For scenery and Barcelona, Catalonia. For guaranteed winter sun, the Canary Islands.

When is the best time for a golf trip to Spain?

October to May on the Costa del Sol and around Valencia, April–June and September–October in Catalonia, and November to March in the Canary Islands.

Do I need a handicap certificate to play golf in Spain?

Many clubs ask for one at check-in, especially the more exclusive courses. Bring your home club card or federation certificate. See how to get a golf handicap in Spain.
